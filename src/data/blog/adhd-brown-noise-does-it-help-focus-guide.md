**ADHD brown noise** is everywhere on TikTok and YouTube, with millions of views and big promises. People say it "switches their brain on" or finally makes the study session, work block or cleaning marathon possible. So is it a real focus tool or just a very relaxing hum?

This guide covers what brown noise is, what the research on noise and ADHD actually shows, how it differs from white and pink noise, and how to run a fair test on your own brain.

> **Pro-tip from ADHD m8:** Treat brown noise as an experiment, not a cure. If it helps, great. If it doesn't, you've lost 10 minutes, not your personality.

_This post is general information, not medical advice. Talk to your doctor or clinician about ADHD treatment._

---

### What Is Brown Noise?

Brown noise is a deep, rumbling sound with more energy in low frequencies than high ones. It sounds like a strong waterfall, a jet engine heard from inside the plane, or heavy rain on a roof. It is not the same as white noise, which is brighter and hissier.

The "brown" in the name comes from Brownian motion, not from the colour. Think of it as the bass-heavy cousin in the noise family.

| Noise type  | Sounds like                        | Frequency balance                                      |
| ----------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| White noise | TV static, a fan, a hiss           | Equal energy across frequencies, so it sounds bright   |
| Pink noise  | Steady rainfall, rustling leaves   | More bass than white, softer to the ear                |
| Brown noise | Waterfall, distant thunder, rumble | Strongest bass, the deepest and smoothest of the three |

### Does Brown Noise Help ADHD Focus?

Some research suggests steady background noise can improve attention and memory in people with ADHD, but there is no strong evidence that brown noise specifically works better than other noise types. The honest answer is "maybe, for some people."

The most-cited study is a 2007 paper by Söderlund and colleagues, [Listen to the noise: noise is beneficial for cognitive performance in ADHD](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=Listen+to+the+noise+noise+is+beneficial+for+cognitive+performance+in+ADHD), which found children with ADHD performed better on memory tasks with moderate white noise, while non-ADHD children did slightly worse. Later reviews have found a small average benefit for attention but note that studies are small, vary a lot, and mostly test white noise rather than brown.

In short, the science is promising, not settled. The viral claims run well ahead of the evidence.

### Why Might Noise Help the ADHD Brain?

The leading idea is that background noise can add just enough stimulation to help an under-aroused ADHD brain stay engaged. This is sometimes called the **moderate brain arousal model**.

ADHD is linked to lower baseline dopamine activity, so quiet, boring tasks can leave the brain hunting for something more interesting. Steady noise may fill that gap so your attention stops wandering off to check the fridge. Researchers also point to a mechanism called stochastic resonance, where a small amount of background "noise" helps a weak signal stand out.

There's a second, more practical benefit: **masking**. A constant rumble covers sudden sounds like a neighbour's drill, a notification ping or coworkers chatting, which is a big deal if you're distracted by every sound. ### Brown Noise vs White Noise vs Pink Noise for ADHD

There is no proven winner among noise colours for ADHD, so choose based on what your ears can tolerate for an hour. Most ADHD adults report one of these patterns:

- **Brown noise:** Best if you find white noise harsh or tiring. The low rumble feels smooth and "full."
- **White noise:** Best at masking high-pitched sounds like voices and keyboards. Some find it scratchy.
- **Pink noise:** A good middle ground, and the type with the most sleep research behind it.

Because most of the lab research used white noise, white is the safest evidence-based starting point. Brown is the popular favourite because it feels gentler.

> **Pro-tip from ADHD m8:** If the noise itself becomes something you notice, it's too loud or the wrong colour. Good focus noise should fade into the background within a minute.

### How to Test Brown Noise for ADHD Focus

You can find out whether brown noise helps you in about a week with a simple A/B test. This beats guessing, and it fits the "experiment, don't commit" approach that works well for ADHD brains.

#### Step 1: Pick a repeatable task

Choose something you do often, such as answering emails, studying or writing. Use the same type of task each time.

#### Step 2: Alternate conditions

Do one 25-minute block with brown noise and the next with silence or your normal music. Switch every day, so time of day doesn't skew results.

#### Step 3: Score it

After each block, rate focus from 1 to 5 and write down how many times you drifted. A sticky note is enough. Pair it with a timer such as the [10-3 vibration method](/posts/adhd-focus-timer-smartwatch-vibration-10-3-method).

#### Step 4: Check the pattern

If brown noise wins most days, keep it. If scores are equal, use whichever is cheaper and less annoying.

### Volume, Headphones and Safety

Keep brown noise at a moderate volume, ideally no louder than a quiet conversation, and take breaks. The World Health Organization advises limiting exposure to loud sound, and noise-related hearing damage is a real risk with long headphone sessions at high volume.

Some practical settings:

- **Volume:** Around 50-60% of max on headphones, or low enough to talk over comfortably.
- **Headphones:** Over-ear or open-back are more comfortable for long sessions than tight earbuds.
- **Infants and bedrooms:** Don't place sound machines close to a baby's head or run them at high volume all night.
- **Tinnitus or ear conditions:** Ask an audiologist before trying long listening sessions.

### When Brown Noise Doesn't Help

Brown noise can backfire if your task needs verbal thinking, if you're sensitive to low rumbling sounds, or if you hope it will fix focus on its own. Some people find it irritating or even anxiety-provoking, and that is a valid result.

It also doesn't replace the basics. If your focus problems are constant, they deserve a proper look, whether that's sleep, medication or support. ### Key Takeaways

- Brown noise is a deep, low-frequency sound with more bass than white or pink noise.
- A 2007 study by Söderlund and colleagues found moderate white noise improved memory task performance in children with ADHD.
- There is no strong evidence that brown noise works better than white or pink noise for ADHD focus.
- Background noise may help by adding stimulation and masking sudden distracting sounds.
- A one-week A/B test, alternating brown noise and silence, is the most reliable way to learn whether it works for you.
- Keep volume moderate, since long sessions at high volume can damage hearing.

---

### Conclusion

Brown noise is a cheap, low-risk experiment that helps some ADHD brains and does nothing for others. It's a tool for your toolbox, not a treatment, and the viral hype outruns the research.
