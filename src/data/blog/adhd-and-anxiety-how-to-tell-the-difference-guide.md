**ADHD and anxiety** are two of the most commonly confused conditions in adults, and they frequently show up together. You can't relax, your mind races, you can't concentrate, you can't sleep. Is that ADHD, anxiety, or both?

If you've been asking "ADHD or anxiety, how do I tell?", this guide breaks down the overlap, the key differences, and what treatment looks like when you're dealing with both. I'm not a doctor, so use this to prepare for a proper conversation with one, not to self-diagnose.

> **Pro-tip from ADHD m8:** Before reading on, grab your phone and jot down your top three "worst" symptoms right now. You'll use them in the "Questions to Ask" section, and future-you will thank present-you.

---

### Why ADHD and Anxiety Get Mixed Up

ADHD and anxiety get mixed up because they share many surface symptoms: poor concentration, restlessness, irritability, sleep trouble, and feeling overwhelmed. A clinician looking at a single symptom often can't tell which condition is driving it.

Consider "I can't focus at work." In ADHD, that happens because the brain struggles to regulate attention. In anxiety, it happens because worry hijacks attention. Same symptom, different engine.

This is also why many adults, especially women, are treated for anxiety for years before anyone considers ADHD. Our [adult ADHD diagnosis guide](/posts/adult-adhd-diagnosis-guide) calls this the comorbidity trap: the anxiety gets treated while the ADHD underneath keeps causing the chaos.

---

### How Common Is It to Have Both?

Having both ADHD and anxiety is very common. Research on adults with ADHD suggests that roughly half meet criteria for an anxiety disorder at some point in their lives, which is well above the rate in the general population. The [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/adhd/) notes that anxiety is among the most frequent co-occurring conditions with ADHD.

Part of the reason is practical. Living with unreliable attention, missed deadlines, and years of criticism gives anyone good reasons to feel anxious. In many people, the anxiety is a **consequence** of untreated ADHD, not a separate problem.

---

### ADHD or Anxiety: Key Differences

ADHD and anxiety differ mainly in what drives the symptom: ADHD is a pattern of attention and self-regulation difficulty that starts in childhood, while anxiety is a pattern of excessive worry about threat or the future. The [National Institute of Mental Health](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/anxiety-disorders) describes anxiety disorders as involving persistent, hard-to-control fear or worry.

| Question                      | More like ADHD                                | More like anxiety                          |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| When did it start?            | Childhood, across many settings               | Often later, or tied to specific stressors |
| Why can't I focus?            | My attention drifts to whatever's interesting | My mind is stuck on a worry                |
| What does my mind do?         | Jumps between unrelated ideas                 | Loops on "what if" scenarios               |
| Why do I avoid tasks?         | I can't get started or sustain it             | I'm afraid of doing it badly               |
| Does it ease when interested? | Yes, hyperfocus can kick in                   | Not really, the worry stays                |
| Physical signs                | Fidgeting, restless energy                    | Racing heart, tight chest, sweating        |

Treat this table as a conversation starter, not a test. Real life is messier, and you can tick boxes in both columns.

### How ADHD Anxiety Feeds Itself

ADHD and anxiety can create a loop where each makes the other worse. It often goes like this:

- ADHD causes a missed deadline or forgotten commitment
- Anxiety rises: "I'm going to mess up again"
- Worry eats working memory, so focus drops further
- Avoidance or [ADHD paralysis](/posts/adhd-paralysis-how-to-get-unstuck-guide) kicks in
- Another thing slips, and the cycle restarts

Poor sleep sits in the middle of this loop. Anxiety keeps you awake, and a tired brain is worse at both attention and emotional regulation. Our [ADHD sleep hygiene guide](/posts/adhd-and-sleep-hygiene-science-backed-guide) covers how to break that part of the chain.

> **Pro-tip from ADHD m8:** Notice whether your anxiety peaks around deadlines and messy admin. If so, a lot of it may be "ADHD tax" anxiety, and fixing the system (reminders, body doubling, smaller steps) can lower it more than breathing exercises alone.

### Can Anxiety Be Mistaken for ADHD, or ADHD for Anxiety?

Yes, in both directions. Anxiety can look like ADHD because worry consumes attention. ADHD can look like anxiety because constant lateness and overwhelm create real stress and visible nervousness.

Other conditions can also mimic or overlap with both, including depression, thyroid problems, sleep apnoea, and trauma-related conditions. This is why a thorough assessment matters. A good clinician will ask about your childhood, your history across settings, and when symptoms began, not just what you feel today.

### Treating ADHD With Anxiety

Treating ADHD with anxiety usually means addressing both conditions, often starting with whichever is causing the most impairment. There's no single right order, and it's a decision to make with a qualified professional.

#### Treating the ADHD

For many people, treating ADHD reduces anxiety because the daily chaos driving it settles down. Options include medication, coaching, and behavioural strategies. Our guide to [ADHD medication for adults](/posts/adhd-medication-for-adults-what-to-expect-guide) explains stimulant and non-stimulant options. Clinicians sometimes prefer non-stimulants or careful dosing when anxiety is severe, since stimulants can worsen jitteriness in some people. That is a prescriber's call.

#### Treating the anxiety

Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT) is a well-supported treatment for anxiety, and adapted versions exist for adults with ADHD. Some people also use medication for anxiety. The [NHS overview of generalised anxiety disorder](https://www.nhs.uk/mental-health/conditions/generalised-anxiety-disorder/overview/) outlines the usual options.

#### Daily habits that help both

- **Move your body.** Regular exercise helps both attention and mood, as covered in our [ADHD and exercise guide](/posts/adhd-and-exercise-how-fitness-helps-the-adhd-brain).
- **Protect sleep.** A consistent wind-down matters more than any hack.
- **Externalise worries.** Write them down so working memory isn't doing the storage.
- **Shrink the task.** Anxiety hates vague, giant jobs. Two-minute starts work well.

### Questions to Ask Your Doctor

Going into an appointment with a clear list makes it easier to get a useful assessment. Try these:

- "My symptoms started in childhood. Could ADHD be behind my anxiety?"
- "Could my anxiety be partly a reaction to untreated ADHD?"
- "How would treating one condition affect the other?"
- "Are there medication options that suit someone with both?"
- "What should I track between now and my follow-up?"

---

### When to Seek Help Urgently

See a doctor soon if anxiety is constant, causing panic attacks, or interfering with work, relationships, or sleep. If you feel hopeless or have thoughts of harming yourself, contact local emergency services or a crisis line straight away. You don't have to wait for a perfect appointment.

---

### Key Takeaways

- ADHD and anxiety share symptoms like poor focus, restlessness, and sleep trouble, so they are commonly mistaken for each other.
- Roughly half of adults with ADHD meet criteria for an anxiety disorder at some point, according to research on co-occurring conditions.
- ADHD begins in childhood as a pattern of attention and regulation difficulty, while anxiety centres on persistent worry about threat or the future.
- In many adults, anxiety is partly a consequence of untreated ADHD, so treating ADHD can ease anxiety.
- Treatment for both usually combines therapy such as CBT, lifestyle habits, and sometimes medication, decided with a qualified clinician.

---

### Conclusion

ADHD and anxiety are separate conditions that love to travel together and disguise each other. Sorting out which is driving your symptoms can change the treatment you get, and often your whole day-to-day experience.

You don't need to figure it out alone. A proper assessment can untangle the two.
