If you crave routine but get bored by it, hate noise but seek stimulation, or feel like two people are fighting over the steering wheel, you may be reading about **AuDHD**. It is the informal name for having both ADHD and autism, and many adults only discover it after years of being told their ADHD "doesn't quite fit". This guide explains what AuDHD is, the signs, how diagnosis works and what helps.

---

### What Is AuDHD?

AuDHD is a community term for a person who has both ADHD and autism spectrum disorder (ASD). It is not a separate diagnosis in the DSM-5 or ICD-11. Clinicians diagnose ADHD and autism as two co-occurring conditions.

Until 2013, the DSM did not allow both diagnoses together. The DSM-5 removed that rule, and clinical guidance such as the [NICE ADHD guideline (NG87)](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng87) now recognises that the two often travel together.

> **Pro-tip from ADHD m8:** "AuDHD" is a handy label for understanding yourself, but it is not a medical term. If you want formal support, ask a clinician about assessing both ADHD and autism.

---

### How Common Is ADHD With Autism?

ADHD and autism co-occur far more often than chance would predict. A meta-analysis by [Rong and colleagues (2021)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/?term=Rong+prevalence+ADHD+autism+spectrum+disorder+meta-analysis) estimated that roughly 40% of autistic people also meet criteria for ADHD. Rates in the other direction are lower but still substantial, with estimates varying widely by study and age group.

Researchers also see overlap in genetics and brain development. Neither condition explains the other. They are separate neurodevelopmental conditions that often share a family tree.

This overlap is also why so many adults are diagnosed late. One condition can hide the other, which is a big reason [adult ADHD diagnosis](/posts/adult-adhd-diagnosis-guide) can feel confusing.

---

### Why Does AuDHD Feel Like a Tug of War?

AuDHD feels contradictory because ADHD and autism often pull in opposite directions. ADHD tends to seek novelty and stimulation, while autism tends to seek sameness and predictability. Living with both can mean wanting two opposite things at the same moment.

Here are common clashes:

- **Routine**: you need structure to cope, but you get bored and abandon it within weeks
- **Stimulation**: you seek excitement, yet a noisy room leaves you shattered
- **Spontaneity**: you impulsively say yes to plans, then melt down when the plan changes
- **Focus**: you [hyperfocus](/posts/adhd-hyperfocus-how-to-use-it-and-stop-it-guide) on special interests, but cannot start a dull task

> **Pro-tip from ADHD m8:** When you feel torn, ask which brain is talking: "I need novelty" or "I need predictability". Naming the voice makes the next move easier to choose.

### What Are the Signs of AuDHD in Adults?

AuDHD in adults shows up as a mix of ADHD signs (inattention, impulsivity, restlessness) and autism signs (social differences, sensory sensitivity, need for routine). No single sign proves it. Look for a consistent pattern from childhood onwards.

#### Signs from the ADHD side

- Trouble starting and finishing tasks, even ones you care about
- Time blindness and chronic lateness (see [ADHD time blindness](/posts/the-mystery-of-time-blindness-why-the-adhd-brain-struggles-with-the-future))
- Impulsive decisions, interrupting, or spending (see [ADHD impulse spending](/posts/adhd-impulse-spending-why-it-happens-and-how-to-stop-guide))
- Restlessness, fidgeting and mental noise

#### Signs from the autism side

- Sensory sensitivity to noise, light, textures or smells
- Strong preference for routines, with distress when plans change
- Social interaction that feels like effort, such as decoding tone, small talk or unspoken rules
- Intense, long-lasting special interests
- Literal thinking or a very direct communication style

#### Signs that sit in the overlap

- Emotional overwhelm and shutdowns
- Exhaustion after socialising
- Repetitive movement or [stimming](/posts/adhd-stimming-in-adults-signs-types-and-what-helps-guide)
- Years of [masking](/posts/adhd-masking-in-adults-signs-costs-and-how-to-unmask-guide) to appear "normal"

### Is It ADHD, Autism or Both?

Telling ADHD and autism apart is hard because many behaviours look alike on the surface. The reason behind the behaviour is usually what differs.

| Behaviour                  | Often ADHD                         | Often autism                                |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Interrupting               | Impulsive, blurting out thoughts   | Missing the cue for when to speak           |
| Avoiding social events     | Forgetting, or feeling overwhelmed | Social effort and sensory load drain energy |
| Sensory sensitivity        | Can come and go with stress        | Usually lifelong and consistent             |
| Difficulty with change     | Dislikes boredom, adapts quickly   | Distress when routines are disrupted        |
| Intense interest in topics | Fades when novelty wears off       | Stays deep and stable for years             |

Because of this overlap, a good assessment looks at your whole life story, not a checklist from one afternoon. Our guides on [ADHD sensory overload](/posts/adhd-sensory-overload-adults-signs-and-coping-guide) and [ADD vs ADHD](/posts/add-vs-adhd-whats-the-difference-explained) cover related confusions.

### How Is AuDHD Diagnosed?

AuDHD is diagnosed by assessing ADHD and autism separately, ideally by clinicians who understand both. There is no blood test or scan. Assessment uses clinical interviews, developmental history and standardised tools.

What to expect:

1. **Self-screening first.** Online quizzes can prompt a conversation, but they cannot diagnose. See our [ADHD quiz for adults](/posts/adhd-quiz-for-adults-self-screening-test).
2. **A detailed history.** Clinicians ask about childhood, school, work, relationships and sensory experiences. A parent or sibling's account can help.
3. **Separate assessments.** Some services assess ADHD only, so ask whether they also assess autism, or whether a referral is needed.
4. **A written report.** This can support workplace adjustments. See [ADHD workplace accommodations](/posts/adhd-workplace-accommodations-guide).

The [NHS autism overview](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/autism/) and the [NIMH page on ADHD](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/topics/attention-deficit-hyperactivity-disorder-adhd) are useful starting points for the official descriptions of each condition.

### What Helps With AuDHD?

What helps AuDHD is support for both sets of needs at once, rather than treating each in isolation. A strategy that only suits ADHD (constant novelty) can overload autistic traits, and the reverse is also true.

#### Build flexible routines

Rigid schedules tend to break and flexible ones tend to survive. Fix a few anchor points, such as wake time, meals and bedtime, and leave the rest loose. Our [ADHD morning routine](/posts/adhd-morning-routine-for-adults-that-actually-works) guide shows how.

#### Protect your sensory budget

Plan for noise, crowds and bright light the way you would plan for money. Noise-cancelling headphones, quiet breaks and an exit plan reduce the toll. [Brown noise](/posts/adhd-brown-noise-does-it-help-focus-guide) helps some people.

#### Use your special interests on purpose

Special interests are a powerful source of motivation and calm. Use them as rewards, as a way into dull tasks, or as a career direction. See [ADHD careers](/posts/the-adhd-career-guide-finding-your-niche-and-avoiding-the-boredom-trap).

#### Plan recovery time

Social effort and masking drain energy fast, so schedule downtime after demanding events. Without it, [ADHD burnout](/posts/adhd-burnout-symptoms-and-recovery-guide) is a real risk.

#### Talk to a clinician about treatment

ADHD medication may help some autistic adults, but responses vary and side effects differ from person to person. Discuss options with a doctor who knows both conditions rather than adjusting anything alone.

### Key Takeaways

- AuDHD is an informal term for having both ADHD and autism, which are diagnosed as two separate conditions.
- The DSM-5 (2013) allowed ADHD and autism to be diagnosed together, and a 2021 meta-analysis estimated about 40% of autistic people also meet ADHD criteria.
- ADHD often seeks novelty while autism often seeks predictability, which creates the "tug of war" many AuDHD adults describe.
- One condition can mask the other, so late diagnosis is common and a full life-history assessment matters.
- Effective support covers both: flexible routines, a sensory budget, planned recovery time and clinician-guided treatment.

---

### Conclusion

AuDHD explains why so many adults feel like a bundle of contradictions. You are not inconsistent or dramatic. You are running two sets of needs that sometimes disagree, and the answer is a plan that respects both.
