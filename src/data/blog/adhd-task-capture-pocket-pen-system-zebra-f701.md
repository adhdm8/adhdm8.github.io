You're halfway through an email when you remember you need to book the dentist. You tell yourself you'll do it in a minute. Ten minutes later, the thought has completely left the building.

That's not laziness. That's **ADHD task capture** failing at the one moment it matters: the few seconds before a thought disappears.

This post is a tiny, low-friction system for catching those thoughts. It needs one pen, one pad, and about two minutes a day.

---

### Why ADHD Brains Drop Tasks Before You Can Act on Them

ADHD brains drop tasks because working memory, the mental sticky note that holds a thought while you do something else, is one of the executive functions most affected by ADHD. [CHADD's overview of executive function](https://chadd.org/about-adhd/executive-function/) lists working memory among the skills that can be harder to rely on with ADHD.

In practice, that sounds like:

- **Why do I forget tasks as soon as I think of them with ADHD?** Because the thought was never stored anywhere except a fragile short-term buffer.
- **Why do I remember it at 2am and not at 2pm?** Because the distraction that bumped it out is gone.
- **Why does "I'll remember" never work?** Because the plan itself relies on the exact function that's unreliable.

The fix isn't trying harder. It's moving the thought out of your head in under five seconds. That's what the **capture-first rule** is for.

---

### The Capture-First Rule: Write Now, Sort Later

The capture-first rule means every task, idea, or worry gets written down the moment it appears, with no sorting, scheduling, or judging at that stage.

Sorting is where ADHD capture systems die. If you have to decide _which list_, _what priority_, or _which app_ before you can write, the thought is already gone.

So the capture step has exactly one job:

- Write it in a few words
- Don't polish it
- Don't act on it (yet)
- Go back to what you were doing

That last point matters. The point of **pen and paper capture for ADHD** is to protect your focus on the current task, not to start a new one. The thought is safe, so you can let it go.

> **Pro-tip from ADHD m8:** Write the _verb_ first. "Book dentist" beats "dentist" because future-you doesn't have to decode what the scribble meant.

### The Three-Place Setup: Pocket, Desk, Bedside

A capture system only works if the pen is within arm's reach when the thought hits, so the setup is three fixed spots, each with its own pen and pad.

#### Place 1: Pocket (or Bag)

A clip-on pen and a small pad or folded index cards. This covers walking, commuting, queues, and the "oh no, I forgot to..." moment.

#### Place 2: Desk

A pad that never leaves the desk, with the pen resting on it. This is for the thoughts that interrupt deep work. Instead of opening a new tab "just to check", you write the thought and stay put.

#### Place 3: Bedside

Racing thoughts at night are a classic. A pad and pen next to the bed lets you empty your head without turning on a bright screen. If sleep is a recurring struggle, our [ADHD sleep hygiene guide](/posts/adhd-and-sleep-hygiene-science-backed-guide/) covers the wider picture.

The key rule: **one pen per place, never moved.** The pen's home is the pad. If you have to look for it, the system is already broken.

### The 2-Minute Daily Sort

The daily sort is a two-minute pass where you turn everything you captured into a short, actionable list, and it's what turns capture from a pile of scribbles into a working system.

Do it once a day, at a consistent anchor point such as your first coffee or shutting your laptop. Then work through the pad:

1. **Do it now** if it takes under two minutes
2. **Move it** to your calendar or main to-do list if it needs a time
3. **Cross it out** if it no longer matters
4. **Rewrite it** at the top of tomorrow's page if it's still important

Then tear off or cross through the old page. A clean page is a small win, and it signals that the capture pad is empty and ready.

### Common Ways This Breaks (and Quick Fixes)

Any capture system will fail sometimes. Here's how to recover without shame:

- **You captured but never sorted.** Skip the guilt. Do one big sort, then restart the two-minute habit tomorrow.
- **The pen went missing.** That's normal. Replace it and put it back in its home spot.
- **The pad is full of unreadable scribbles.** Write fewer words and lead with the verb.
- **You forgot to capture at all.** Treat it as data: which place was missing a pen? Add one there.

The goal is a system that survives a bad week, not one that needs a perfect streak.

### FAQ: ADHD Task Capture

**Q:** What is ADHD task capture?
**A:** ADHD task capture is the habit of writing down tasks, ideas, and worries the instant they appear, so working memory doesn't have to hold them.

**Q:** Is pen and paper better than a phone app for capture?
**A:** For many people with ADHD, yes, because paper has no notifications or extra steps. If an app genuinely works for you, use it; the rule that matters is capturing in under five seconds.

**Q:** How many places should I keep a capture pad?
**A:** Three is a practical starting point: pocket, desk, and bedside. Add more only if you notice gaps.

**Q:** How long should the daily sort take?
**A:** About two minutes. If it's taking longer, you're probably capturing too much detail.
