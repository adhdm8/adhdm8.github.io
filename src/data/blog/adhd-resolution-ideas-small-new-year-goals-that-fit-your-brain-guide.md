ADHD resolution ideas work best when they are tiny, concrete and easy to restart after a miss. "Get healthy" gives an ADHD brain nothing to grab. "Walk to the end of the street after lunch" does.

If you're an adult with ADHD staring at a blank January page, this guide gives you 12 realistic resolution ideas, a quick way to choose between them, and a plan for the February slump. For the deeper science of why big resolutions collapse, see our companion guide on [ADHD New Year's resolutions](/posts/adhd-new-years-resolutions-why-they-fail-and-goals-that-stick-guide).

---

### What Makes a Good Resolution for an ADHD Brain?

A good ADHD resolution is small, specific, visible and rewarding right now. It has a clear cue, takes under ten minutes and survives a bad week.

Use this four-part test before you commit:

- **Specific:** You can picture yourself doing it.
- **Small:** The first step takes under two minutes.
- **Cued:** It's attached to something you already do daily.
- **Rewarding:** Something feels good immediately, not in June.

> **Pro-tip from ADHD m8:** If a resolution needs motivation to start, it's too big. Shrink it until starting feels almost silly.

---

### Why Do Year-Long Goals Feel So Slippery With ADHD?

Year-long goals feel slippery with ADHD because ADHD time blindness makes distant deadlines feel abstract, so January's effort seems pointless until it's too late. Research by Dr Russell Barkley describes ADHD as a difficulty acting on time that isn't happening now.

That's why thinking in weeks works better than thinking in years. Our [ADHD time blindness guide](/posts/the-mystery-of-time-blindness-why-the-adhd-brain-struggles-with-the-future) explains the mechanism.

### 12 ADHD Resolution Ideas for Adults

Each idea below is small enough to start tomorrow. Pick one, not twelve.

#### Daily Habits

1. **Take medication at a fixed cue**, if you're prescribed it. Pair it with coffee or brushing your teeth.
2. **Do a two-minute morning launch:** water, meds, glance at today's plan. Build it into your [ADHD morning routine](/posts/adhd-morning-routine-for-adults-that-actually-works).
3. **Set a bedtime alarm, not a bedtime rule.** It helps with [revenge bedtime procrastination](/posts/adhd-revenge-bedtime-procrastination-why-you-stay-up-guide).

#### Work and Money

4. **Do one 25-minute focus block a day** with a timer or a [body doubling session](/posts/body-doubling-for-adhd-best-apps-and-how-it-works).
5. **Run a five-minute Friday reset:** clear the desk, note next week's top three tasks.
6. **Add a 24-hour wait rule** for non-essential purchases to tame [impulse spending](/posts/adhd-impulse-spending-why-it-happens-and-how-to-stop-guide).

#### Body and Mind

7. **Move for 10 minutes** on days a full workout feels impossible. Exercise has real benefits for the ADHD brain, as covered in our [ADHD and exercise guide](/posts/adhd-and-exercise-how-fitness-helps-the-adhd-brain).
8. **Eat one planned meal a day**, even if it's the same meal.
9. **Schedule one low-stakes social plan a month.**

#### Home and Life

10. **Reset one surface** (kitchen counter, desk) before bed.
11. **Keep a "one place" spot** for keys, wallet and phone.
12. **Try one new tool a month**, such as an app from our [best ADHD apps guide](/posts/best-adhd-apps-for-adults-guide).

### How Do You Choose Just One Resolution?

Choosing one resolution with ADHD is easier if you score each idea quickly. Give each option 1-3 points for how much you want it, how small it is and how often you'd naturally do it.

Pick the highest scorer. Work on it for four weeks before adding anything else, because a short list protects your working memory (see our [ADHD working memory guide](/posts/adhd-working-memory-why-you-forget-and-what-helps-guide)).

---

### How Do You Keep Going When the Novelty Fades?

Resolutions stick when you build in rewards and restarts. Novelty wears off after two or three weeks, so plan for it.

- **Add a reward you can feel today:** a favourite podcast on the walk.
- **Use "never miss twice":** one miss is normal, two starts a new habit.
- **Swap the version, not the goal:** change the playlist, location or timing.

Our [ADHD dopamine menu guide](/posts/adhd-dopamine-menu-how-to-build-one-guide) helps you build a list of rewards to rotate through.

### FAQ: ADHD Resolution Ideas

**Q: What are good New Year's resolutions for someone with ADHD?**
A: Small, specific habits tied to an existing cue, such as taking medication with coffee, a 10-minute walk or a five-minute Friday reset.

**Q: How many resolutions should I set with ADHD?**
A: One to three is plenty. One goal at a time lowers the load on working memory.

**Q: What if I forget my resolution by February?**
A: Treat it as data. Make the goal smaller, add a visible cue and restart the same day.

### Key Takeaways

- Good ADHD resolution ideas are small, specific, cued and immediately rewarding.
- ADHD time blindness makes year-long goals feel abstract, so weekly goals work better than yearly ones.
- Choosing one resolution for four weeks protects working memory better than starting several at once.
- Attaching a new habit to an existing daily cue makes it easier to start.
- A "never miss twice" rule turns a slip into a restart rather than a failure.

---

### Conclusion

You don't need a new personality in January. You need one small goal, a cue and a reward that you can feel today. Pick the idea that makes you slightly curious, not the one that makes you feel guilty.
